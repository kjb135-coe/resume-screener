## Correction — 2026-08-27

Two things in this document are stale.

**The never-escalate figure.** This page reports **0.797**;
`docs/RESULTS_HISTORY.md` elsewhere cites **0.784**. Measured over
`var1`/`var3`/`var4` the answer is **0.813 mean, range 0.784-0.830** —
0.784 was a single run, not the estimate. The arbiter's entire
contribution is **0.027 macro-F1**, inside the 0.051 noise band
(`docs/VARIANCE.md`).

**The policies swept here no longer describe the shipped gate.**
Escalation now requires a third condition: the panel mean must sit within
`ESCALATION_MARGIN` (0.5) of a cutoff, because the arbiter cannot cross a
line further away than it has ever moved a score. That took escalation
from 46.9% to 12.3% with macro-F1 unchanged.

The sweep's own methodological limit still stands and is why these tables
could not have found that: for a candidate the recorded run did *not*
escalate there is no counterfactual arbiter output, so every spread
policy reuses the same predictions and reports the same macro-F1. The
column that separated policies was always cost.

Numbers above are kept as recorded.
