# Scoring rubric — anchored to docs/job_description.md, not a generic template

This text is the cacheable prefix shared by every Tier 1 panel call for a
given job description. It does not change per-candidate, which is what
makes prompt caching effective here.

This posting is explicit that it wants a solutions-engineer profile, not
a research profile: hands-on building of agentic systems (memory, tools,
orchestration), production deployment ("not demos or prototypes"),
integration into real business systems, and client-facing communication
balanced against engineering rigor. Score against that profile
specifically, not against a generic AI-engineer checklist.

Score the candidate 0-10 on each of the three dimensions below, and
report a confidence (0-1) alongside each score, not just the score
itself.

1. **Production reality** — does the evidence describe systems that
   shipped and are used, or research, coursework, and proof-of-concept
   work that stopped at the demo stage? This posting explicitly
   distinguishes "not demos or prototypes, but systems used in
   production" — weight this heavily. A resume can be technically
   impressive and still score low here if nothing describes production
   ownership: on-call, monitoring, handling real user load, iterating
   after launch.
2. **Technical and integration depth** — hands-on evidence of building
   agentic systems (memory, tools, orchestration specifically — the
   posting's own language), LLM/GenAI work, and integrating AI into
   real APIs, business systems, or automation platforms, on cloud-native
   or hybrid infrastructure. Evidence must describe what they built, not
   a skills line — a tool named without a sentence describing what it
   did is not evidence.
3. **Client-facing and cross-functional signal** — evidence of
   explaining technical work to non-technical audiences, working across
   engineering/sales/delivery, or direct client/customer engagement.
   This is the hardest dimension to find evidence for in a typical
   resume — its absence is not automatically disqualifying, but its
   presence is a genuine differentiator this specific posting asks for
   by name.

Every claim you make must cite a direct quote from the candidate's
extracted evidence. If the evidence doesn't support a claim, say so
rather than inferring it — do not give credit for what a skills line
implies if no evidence backs it.
